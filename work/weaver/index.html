01 · The challenge

Slow knitwear,
a fast-fashion store.

Weaver knits undyed merino and alpaca in monthly batches of 60 pieces. Most shoppers find it on Instagram and arrive on a phone.

The store fought the product: a 2018 theme, a clip-art logo, a prize wheel, and countdowns on pieces knit after you order them.

02 · The new brand

Keep the name.
Lose the yarn ball.

“Weaver” was a good name stuck in a craft-fair logo. The Brand Refresh kept the name and gave it the voice of an atelier: a Bodoni italic, a light Gill Sans line that says what it is, and nothing else.

Palette

Fleece, oatmeal, undyed grey and walnut from the wool; harbor navy for type; madder only where something is scarce.

Type

Newsreader and Instrument Sans, self-hosted on the store: 76 KB for four files.

Voice

Say the date, name the yarn. No exclamation marks, no fake urgency.

03 · The new store

Four pages, one question:
when is it mine?

The home page leads with the open batch and the best seller. Collections filter by fit, shade and what is still open. Product page and cart put the delivery date next to the button.

01

Navigation & menu

41 desktop menu links became 5: Sweaters, Cardigans, Accessories, How batches work and About. The mobile drawer went from 47 links to 7.

02

Batches & the waitlist

The limits are real: 60 pieces a month. The old store hid them behind fake ones. Now every card says “In Batch 15 · ships Dec 1”, and a full size opens a waitlist for the next batch, by email or text.

03

SEO, speed & email

  • Product JSON-LD with an offer per size, shipping and returns
  • Titles under 60 characters; old collections redirect
  • AVIF/WebP crops per screen, no render-blocking scripts
  • Welcome, abandoned-bag and review emails, each with a date in it
